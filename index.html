Metric ladder (all 100% ownership, pre fee/equity split): Operating profit = revenue − all cash operating costs, capex expensed. EBITDA = operating profit + the capex expensed that year added back (this model expenses capex rather than depreciating it, and carries no interest, so operating-profit + capex is a true EBITDA; at defaults Year-5 capex is 0, so EBITDA equals operating profit here but diverges in any capex year). After-tax net = pre-tax − 25% corporate tax (Investor page). Multiple = the sum of every center's own 100% operating profit across all 5 model years ÷ Total Committed — a cumulative multi-year figure, not an annual rate (comparable to a simple gross MOIC, not IRR). Exit Value = Year-5 EBITDA × the Exit Multiple slider (industry-appropriate: healthcare clinics typically trade at 6–12× EV/EBITDA at exit, a proven de-risked platform like this one commanding the higher end — same slider and same EBITDA base as the Investor page). The flagship's actual entitlement is smaller — see "Exit Value — Year 5" and "Implied Investor MOIC at Exit" on the Investor page for the equity-adjusted figures.

The network starts with Istanbul as the flagship — its own clinic plus the network's central functions. Izmir and Ankara come online as satellites in Year 2 (core plan); Bursa and Gaziantep are a Phase 2 option from Year 3. In the committed default each centre is a branch of the flagship: 100% of its results are consolidated, with no management fee. Each can alternatively be modelled as a separate, majority-owned subsidiary with a local partner — the flagship then earns a management fee on satellite revenue plus its share of satellite profit. This dashboard models Istanbul as the flagship — its own clinic plus the network's management company. Additional cities (Izmir, Ankara, Bursa, Gaziantep) come online as satellites. In the committed default they are branches of the flagship: funded by Stage 2 of this round, 100% of their results consolidated into the flagship, no management fee and no local investors. Each can alternatively be modelled as a separate, majority-owned subsidiary with local investors — the flagship then earns a management fee on satellite revenue plus an equity share of satellite profit.
